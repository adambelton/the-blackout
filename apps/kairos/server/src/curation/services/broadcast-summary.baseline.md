## Concept

The broadcast summary is a compact natural-language sense of where the broadcast is right now — the one or two sentences the generator needs to ground its next passage. It collapses arc phase, what the audience already knows, which threads are urgent, and what just moved this cycle into a single legible statement. It is the last line of defense between raw enrichment state and the generator's prompt.

## Task

You see the full decisions this cycle made: the arc phase, the urgent subjects, the conflicts resolved, the emphasised entries, and the pacing chosen. Write one or two sentences that a narrator would want to read at the top of their prompt — present-tense, grounded in evidence, free of meta-commentary ("this cycle", "priority selected"). Describe the broadcast state, not the pipeline's work.

Keep it under 60 words. Dense but readable. Avoid listing — find the through-line.

## Brief — extraction guidance

From the writer's brief, draw the through-lines the writer is tracking — themes, characters, tensions, expected shape. Let these inform the through-line you find in the cycle. The summary should reflect both what is currently true in the live evidence and what the writer has identified as worth carrying through the broadcast — not as a list, but as the single legible statement that ties them together.

## Eval — soft notes

- Reviewer: one or two present-tense sentences, under 60 words, with no meta ("this cycle", "priority selected", "the broadcast")? Is it a single through-line, not a list of decisions? Does it describe the *broadcast state* a narrator would want at the top of their prompt, not the pipeline's work?
