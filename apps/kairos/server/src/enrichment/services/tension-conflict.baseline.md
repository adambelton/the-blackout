## Concept

Tension and conflict is the pressure between opposing forces and the stakes that make their collision matter. A conflict carries at least one pole pushing against something — another party, a constraint, a deadline, a material outcome on the line.

## What counts as a subject

A subject is a specific conflict or locus of stake — typically a pair or more of opposing parties, or a single stake the scene is pressing against. Each distinct conflict is its own subject. Do not collapse multiple independent conflicts into one.

## Reading shape

Each reading has: `poles` (the parties or forces in opposition — one-element array is valid when the conflict is a single party against a standalone stake); `stake` (one phrase for what's at risk); `level` (low | moderate | high | critical); `trajectory` (escalating | easing | holding). Choose the closest discrete level.

## Brief — extraction guidance

From the writer's brief, draw any tension lines the writer has named — between parties, between characters, around stakes the writer has identified. Use these to inform what counts as a meaningful tension subject and what raises the stakes. The brief tells you what the writer has identified as conflicts; live evidence determines when and how those conflicts are actually advancing.

## Brief — initialisation guidance

From this brief, lift the tension lines the writer has named — clashes between parties, between characters, between an actor and a constraint, between a side and a deadline. For each, mint a stable subject id, a clear label, and an initial reading: `poles` (the parties or forces in opposition — one-element array when the conflict is a single party against a standalone stake); `stake` (one phrase for what's on the line as the writer frames it); `level` (low | moderate | high | critical — the writer's intensity); `trajectory` (typically `holding` at the broadcast's start unless the writer explicitly signals an escalation already underway). Aim for 2-5 conflicts; only include tensions the brief substantively foregrounds.

## Eval — soft notes

- Reviewer: is each subject a distinct conflict with a real stake, not a vague "tension"? Are independent conflicts kept separate rather than collapsed into one? Does `level` / `trajectory` move only on genuine escalation or easing — not on the same conflict grinding on at the same intensity?
