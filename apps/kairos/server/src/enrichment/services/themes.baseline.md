## Concept

Themes are the meaning the broadcast is carrying — the things the story is about beneath the surface of events. Themes move slowly: they emerge from patterns of events, become established when repeatedly reinforced, and fade when the story moves past them.

## What counts as a subject

A subject is one distinct theme — a meaning, a moral throughline, a shape the story is pressing toward. Each theme is tracked as its own subject. Resist multiplying subjects; a broadcast typically has only a handful of live themes at a time.

## Reading shape

Each reading has: `description` (one sentence stating the theme); `weight` (low | moderate | high — how much of the story this theme is carrying); `status` (emerging | established | fading). A theme should be reported only when the new entries genuinely move its weight or status.

## Brief — extraction guidance

From the writer's brief, draw any themes the writer has named — what they want this broadcast to be about, the recurring concerns or motifs they're tracking. Treat brief-named themes as primary candidates when live evidence touches them. New themes can still emerge purely from live evidence; brief-named themes take priority when the evidence supports them.

## Brief — initialisation guidance

From this brief, lift the themes the writer has committed the broadcast to track — the meanings beneath the surface, the moral throughlines, the things the writer keeps returning to. For each, mint a stable subject id, a clear human label, and an initial reading: `description` quoting or paraphrasing the writer's framing in one sentence; `weight` set to `low` or `moderate` depending on how central the writer treats the theme; `status` typically `emerging` — these are themes the broadcast is poised to carry, not yet ones the audience has heard. Aim for 2-5 themes; the brief usually carries fewer truly distinct themes than it initially appears to.

## Eval — soft notes

- Reviewer: are these meanings *beneath* the events, not restatements of them? Do themes move slowly — a single event shouldn't mint a new theme? Is a brief-named theme weighted up only when live evidence actually touches it, and is description drift on a stable weight + status correctly NOT re-fired?
