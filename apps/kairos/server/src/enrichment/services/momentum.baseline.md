## Concept

Momentum is the rate and direction of change in narrative energy. It answers: is something building, holding, or fading? Direction describes how the current reading compares to the subject's expressed (audience-known) baseline; intensity describes how forceful the activity itself feels right now.

## What counts as a subject

A subject is any entity or collective whose narrative energy can be separately assessed — an individual actor in a lead role, a group or side, or the overall scene as a whole. You may track one or several. When no distinct poles emerge, treat the overall scene as a single subject.

## Reading shape

Each reading has two fields: `direction` (rising | stable | falling) — compared to the subject's expressed baseline — and `intensity` (dormant | low | moderate | high | peak). Use discrete levels; pick the closest fit.

## Brief — extraction guidance

From the writer's brief, draw any sense of the broadcast's expected energetic shape — anticipated rises, expected lulls, characters or threads whose momentum the writer is watching. Use these as priors when reading direction and intensity in the live evidence; the brief tells you what kinds of momentum to look for, not what momentum to declare.

## Brief — initialisation guidance

From this brief, lift the entities or collectives whose momentum the writer wants the broadcast to track. For each, mint a stable subject id, a clear human label, and an initial reading: `direction` (typically `stable` at the broadcast's start unless the writer explicitly signals an established trajectory carried in from before) and `intensity` (dormant | low | moderate | high | peak — the energy the writer attributes to this subject going in). Aim for 1-3 subjects; momentum at the start is usually under-defined and live evidence will quickly shape it.

## Eval — soft notes

- Reviewer: is `direction` read against the subject's *expressed* baseline rather than as an absolute? Does a reading re-fire on a single intensity step (drift), or only on a genuine shift (direction change, or intensity moving ≥ 2 levels)? When no distinct poles emerge, is the scene treated as a single subject?
